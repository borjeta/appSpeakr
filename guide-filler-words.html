How to cut filler words when you speak: a practical guide

Filler words are words or sounds we repeat without meaning to while we speak ("um", "uh", "like", "you know", "basically"). Using a few is normal; the problem starts when they bury your message or take away from your confidence. This guide summarizes a simple method to measure and reduce them.

Why do they happen?

They usually show up when we are searching for the next word, when nerves speed us up, or when silence feels uncomfortable: they fill the gap while we think. That is why they are not fixed by willpower in the middle of a talk, but by practicing beforehand.

A 5-step method

  1. Measure your starting point. Record 2 minutes talking about a topic you know and count the filler words per minute (or use an app that estimates them). Without a starting number you will not know whether you are improving.
  2. Swap the filler for a pause. Replace the "um" with a short silence. It feels long at first; to listeners it usually sounds natural.
  3. Prepare openings and transitions. Many fillers appear when you start an idea or change topic. Rehearse the first sentence of each section out loud.
  4. Slow down. Speaking more slowly gives you time to think of the next sentence. As a reference, Speakr marks a target range of 130 to 160 words per minute; the right pace depends on the context and the person.
  5. Repeat and compare. Practice a little and often (1 or 2 minutes) and compare week to week. The trend matters, not any single practice.

Common filler words

In English: "um", "uh", "like", "you know", "so", "basically", "I mean". In Spanish: "eh", "este", "o sea", "bueno", "¿vale?", "¿sabes?", "básicamente". Everyone has their own; identify yours in your own recordings.
