The STAR method: how to answer job interview questions

The STAR method is a structure for answering behavioral questions, the ones that start with "tell me about a time when...". STAR stands for Situation, Task, Action and Result.

The structure

  • Situation: the context, in one or two sentences.
  • Task: what you had to do, or what had to be achieved.
  • Action: what you did, step by step. It is the most important part and should take the most time.
  • Result: what happened because of what you did, with numbers if you can, and what you learned.

Example (fictional)

Question: "Tell me about a time you had to meet a very tight deadline."

S: "At my previous company, a week before the quarterly report was due, a teammate went on leave." T: "I had to finish their part as well as mine." A: "I prioritized the critical sections, asked another team for the missing data and prepared a draft mid-week to review with my manager." R: "We delivered on time and the report needed two minor corrections instead of the usual five."

Common mistakes

  • Not reaching the result, or not saying what your action achieved.
  • Always saying "we" without making your own role clear.
  • Not quantifying: "it improved a lot" says less than "we cut the time by 20%".
  • Answers that are too long or too short. As a general reference, 1 to 2 minutes per answer.

How to practice it

  1. Prepare five or six real stories you can adapt to several questions (an achievement, a conflict, a mistake, a tight deadline, an initiative).
  2. Rehearse them out loud, not only in your head: it is different.
  3. Record yourself and check whether all four parts appear, whether there are numbers and whether you say "I" where it matters.
